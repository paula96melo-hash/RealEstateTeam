# 05 — Florida Advertising, Fair Housing & Marketing Compliance

> **As of:** November 2026. **This is research context, not legal advice.** Every agent in the system must treat this file as a *gate*: no ad, post, email, text campaign, or lead form ships without passing the checklist in §8. Ambiguity → escalate to the supervising broker / a Florida real estate attorney.
> Always confirm the current text at: flrules.org (Ch. 61J2), leg.state.fl.us (Ch. 475, 501, 689, 718, 760), Palm Beach County Code (Municode), HUD, FCC.

## 1. Licensing basics that affect marketing 🟢
- A Florida **sales associate** may only perform licensed activities **under a registered broker**; all advertising is done on behalf of that brokerage.
- **Post-licensing:** sales associates must complete the **45-hour post-licensing course before their first renewal** (18–24 months after licensure, depending on cycle) or the license becomes null and void (Rule 61J2-3.020). After that: 14 hours CE per 2-year renewal.
- "REALTOR®" is a trademark — only NAR members may use it, and only correctly (all caps or with ®, referring to membership, not as a synonym for "agent").

## 2. FREC advertising rules
### Rule 61J2-10.025 — Advertising 🟢
- **All advertising must be done in a manner that a reasonable person would know they are dealing with a real estate licensee.**
- **The licensed name of the brokerage firm must appear in every real estate advertisement** — including websites, social media profiles/posts, email signatures, yard signs, business cards, digital ads, and video.
- No **false, deceptive, or misleading** advertising (also Fla. Stat. 475.42 / 475.25). Includes unverifiable superlatives ("#1 agent in WPB") unless substantiated, fake "sold" claims, and misleading price/discount claims.
- A licensee's personal name may appear, but **not without the brokerage name**; a sales associate may not advertise as if they were the broker.
- Practical social-media standard: brokerage name on the profile/bio *and* readily visible on property posts; check brokerage policy (many require it on every post).

### Rule 61J2-10.026 — Team or group advertising 🟢
- Team = name or logo used by one or more licensees representing themselves as a team/group.
- Team must operate under the same broker; a **designated licensee** must be filed with the broker as responsible for team ad compliance.
- **Team name may not be in larger print than the brokerage name.**
- Team names should not include words implying a separate brokerage (e.g., "Realty," "Brokerage," "Agency," "Company," "Associates" — confirm against current rule text).

## 3. Fair housing
### Protected classes
| Level | Protected classes |
|---|---|
| **Federal Fair Housing Act** | Race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, disability |
| **Florida Fair Housing Act (Fla. Stat. 760.20–760.37)** | Race, color, national origin, sex, disability (formerly "handicap"), familial status (incl. pregnancy), religion |
| **Palm Beach County Fair Housing Ordinance** (Ord. 90-1, as amended) | Above **plus age, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression** 🟢 (PBC Office of Equal Opportunity) |
| **Source of income** | **Not confirmed** as protected in Palm Beach County (a county Analysis of Impediments recommended adding it). Verify current code. 🔴 |
| **City of West Palm Beach** | May have its own human rights ordinance — **verify** before launch. |

Note: HUD's federal interpretation regarding sexual orientation/gender identity has shifted with administrations; the **county ordinance protects them regardless** — so treat them as protected for all WPB-area marketing.

### Advertising prohibitions (Fla. Stat. 760.23(3); 42 U.S.C. 3604(c))
- No ad, statement, or notice indicating **any preference, limitation, or discrimination** based on a protected class. Applies to listing remarks, social captions, videos, and *conversations*.
- No **steering** (directing buyers toward/away from areas based on protected class) and no **blockbusting** (760.23(5)).
- **Language to avoid / rewrite:** "perfect for families," "family neighborhood," "great for young professionals," "ideal for singles/couples," "mature/adult community" (unless a lawful 55+/62+ housing-for-older-persons community), "exclusive," "safe neighborhood," "quiet neighborhood with no kids," "walking distance to church/synagogue" (as a selling point aimed at a religion), "able-bodied," "no wheelchairs," "English only," nationality/ethnicity descriptors.
- **Describe the property, not the people:** "3 bedrooms, fenced yard, 0.4 mi to [park]" ✅.
- **Schools & crime:** do not characterize schools or neighborhood "safety"; link buyers to official sources (School District of Palm Beach County boundary locator, PBSO/WPBPD crime maps) and let them decide.
- **55+ communities:** may advertise age restriction only if the community qualifies under the Housing for Older Persons Act (HOPA) exemption.
- Include **Equal Housing Opportunity** statement/logo on ads, websites, and print where practical.
- **Images:** use diverse, representative imagery; avoid consistently depicting one demographic.
- **AI tools:** AI copy generators frequently produce fair-housing violations ("perfect for a growing family"). All AI-generated copy must be run through the §8 checklist. Disclose virtual staging / digitally altered images.

### Digital ad targeting
- **Meta (Facebook/Instagram):** housing ads **must** be declared under the **Special Ad Category: Housing**. This removes/limits targeting by age, gender, ZIP-level geography (minimum radius applies), detailed demographic/interest targeting, and blocks Saved Audiences / lookalike-style audiences per Meta policy (Meta's 2022 DOJ settlement). Enforcement is a hard error in the API 🟢.
- **Google Ads:** housing ads are restricted from targeting by age, gender, parental status, marital status, or ZIP code (US housing policy).
- **TikTok, LinkedIn, Nextdoor:** apply similar special-category restrictions — check each platform's housing policy.
- Never use custom audiences built to exclude/include protected classes.

## 4. Calls, texts & email — consent rules
### Federal TCPA (47 U.S.C. 227; 47 CFR 64.1200) 🟢
- **Prior express written consent** required for telemarketing calls/texts to cell phones using an autodialer or artificial/prerecorded voice (incl. AI voice).
- The FCC's **one-to-one consent rule was vacated** by the 11th Circuit (*Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC*, Jan. 2025) and formally removed by FCC rule (Sept. 2025). Lead-gen sites can still be a risk — **best practice is still seller-specific consent naming your brokerage.**
- **Revocation of consent** (FCC rules effective April 2025): consumers may revoke by any reasonable means (e.g., "stop," "quit," "unsubscribe," "cancel," "end," "revoke," "opt out"); honor promptly (within 10 business days under FCC rules).
- **National Do Not Call Registry:** scrub before cold calling/texting; keep an internal DNC list. Statutory damages $500–$1,500 per violation.

### Florida Telephone Solicitation Act — "mini-TCPA" (Fla. Stat. 501.059) 🟢
- Requires prior express written consent for telephonic sales calls/texts using automated systems.
- **2023 amendment (HB 761, ch. 2023-150):** for text messages, a recipient must reply "STOP"; the sender then has **15 days** to cease before a damages suit can be filed. Consent can be shown by checking a box or affirmatively replying to texts. Autodialer definition narrowed.
- **Calling limits (from 2021 amendments — verify current text):** no solicitation calls **before 8 a.m. or after 8 p.m.** (recipient's local time); **no more than 3 calls in 24 hours** on the same subject; caller ID must not be blocked/spoofed.
- **Florida "No Sales Solicitation Calls" list** (administered by FDACS) — scrub in addition to the federal list.

### Email — CAN-SPAM 🟢
- Accurate sender info, non-deceptive subject lines, physical postal address (brokerage), working unsubscribe honored within 10 business days.

### Practical consent standard for this system
Every lead-capture point (website forms, open-house sign-in, landing pages, QR codes) must include **unchecked** checkbox consent language naming the brokerage and the agent, stating calls/texts may use automated technology, that consent is not a condition of purchase, and msg/data rates may apply. Store timestamp, source, IP/form version in the CRM.

## 5. NAR settlement & MLS practice rules 🟢/🟡
- Since **Aug 17, 2024**, MLS participants must have a **written agreement with a buyer before touring** a home (in-person or live virtual). Agreement must state compensation in an **objective, ascertainable, not open-ended** amount; agent may not receive more than the agreement allows.
- Offers of buyer-broker compensation **may not be communicated via the MLS**; can be negotiated off-MLS.
- **Florida Realtors forms:** Exclusive Buyer Brokerage Agreement (No Brokerage Relationship and Single Agent versions), plus showing/pre-touring agreements — use current Form Simplicity versions.
- Florida brokerage relationships (Fla. Stat. 475.278): **transaction broker** is presumed unless single agent or no-brokerage relationship is established; required disclosures still apply.
- Settlement status: appeals reportedly concluded in 2026 (appeal affirmed Aug 2026, rehearing denied Sept 2026) 🔴 — verify.
- **NAR Multiple Listing Options for Sellers (Mar 2025; implement by Sept 30, 2025):** Clear Cooperation Policy remains (listing must be in MLS within 1 business day of public marketing), with new **delayed-marketing exempt** and **office exclusive** options requiring signed seller certification/disclosures. Check **BeachesMLS** rules for local delay periods.

## 6. RESPA (Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act) 🟢
- §8 prohibits giving/receiving **anything of value for referrals** of settlement services (lenders, title, insurance tied to settlement). Co-marketing with lenders must be **paid proportionally** to each party's share of the ad. No "free" CRM or ads paid by a lender in exchange for referrals.

## 7. Florida disclosure topics agents will reference in content
- **Flood disclosure** (Fla. Stat. 689.302, effective Oct 1, 2024): seller must provide the "Flood Disclosure" form at or before contract — awareness of flood damage, flood insurance claims, federal flood assistance. A 2025 bill (SB 948) proposed amendments — verify status.
- **Condo disclosures** (Fla. Stat. Ch. 718): buyer's right to association documents, milestone inspection & SIRS summaries, budgets; rescission periods. HB 913 (2025) changed SIRS/milestone details.
- **Property tax**: Amendment 3 (2026) — avoid giving tax advice; cite official sources/Property Appraiser.
- **Johnson v. Davis** duty to disclose known material defects (FL common law).

## 8. Pre-publish compliance checklist (for every marketing asset)
1. ☐ Brokerage licensed name present and legible (and ≥ team name size)?
2. ☐ Clearly identifies a licensee? Agent name matches license?
3. ☐ No false, misleading, unverifiable claims (stats cited with source/date)?
4. ☐ No protected-class preference, people-descriptions, steering, school/safety characterizations?
5. ☐ Equal Housing Opportunity statement/logo where practical?
6. ☐ Paid social/search ad set to housing special category; no demographic targeting?
7. ☐ "REALTOR®" used correctly (only if member)?
8. ☐ Listing photos/remarks used with listing broker & seller permission; MLS/IDX display rules followed?
9. ☐ Virtual staging / AI-altered images disclosed?
10. ☐ Any call/text campaign: consent on file, DNC (federal + Florida) scrubbed, 8am–8pm, ≤3 calls/24h, opt-out honored?
11. ☐ Email: CAN-SPAM elements present?
12. ☐ Co-marketing with lender/title/insurance: costs split proportionally (RESPA)?
13. ☐ Broker approval obtained if brokerage policy requires?
