### Short answer
**From a due-diligence view, I would not recommend signing this as written.** It is a legal decision for the broker and attorney, but the building facts are clear. The terms move almost every known building risk onto the buyer. They also take away the time and the deposit protections the buyer would need to measure those risks. And they move closing to **Dec 19, 2026**, just ahead of a **Jan 8, 2027** board vote on a proposed special assessment of about **$33,600 per unit**.

### Possible financial exposure to the buyer (sample figures, rough math, not a forecast)
- Proposed assessment: **about $33,600**
- Unfunded SIRS gap of about $1.55M, which works out to **about $24,000 per unit** if split equally. It probably **overlaps** with the concrete assessment, so do not add the two together.
- Milestone repairs: **unknown** until Phase 1 and Phase 2 are done
- Storm deductible share: **about $17,200** if there is a loss
- Deposits at risk after day 5: **$40,000** (about 12.6% of the $318,500 price)

### Top three things to negotiate (for the broker and attorney to frame)
**1. Who pays the special assessment, and when closing happens.**
The seller's wording ("levied on or before Closing," with the buyer taking anything "approved at or after" Jan 8) combines with moving closing up from Jan 15 to Dec 19. Together they put the known $33,600 proposal on the buyer. Options to raise:
- Seller pays any assessment that is *proposed, noticed, or disclosed* before closing.
- Or a price cut or seller credit of about the proposed amount.
- Or an escrow holdback at closing.
- Or closing after the Jan 8 meeting, with a buyer right to cancel if an assessment is approved.

**2. Restore time to review and protect the deposit.**
- 5-day "AS IS" inspection period (buyer offered 15).
- Buyer "acknowledges receipt" of association documents at signing. That only works if they were really delivered and complete. Also, the resale review window was reportedly lengthened to 7 days, excluding weekends and holidays, by HB 913 (secondary source). Whether the statutory rights can be shortened or deemed started this way is a **question for the attorney.**
- A waiver of all claims about structural condition.
- $40k becomes non-refundable on day 5.

Ask for:
- A realistic inspection period
- Document review that starts when complete documents are actually received
- Removal of the structural-claim waiver
- A smaller deposit, or a later point when it becomes non-refundable
- A right to cancel based on the milestone and SIRS findings

**3. Financing protections.**
The counter shortens the loan contingency to 15 days and **waives appraisal**, on a building some lenders have reportedly declined, where no questionnaire has been ordered, reserves are low, and the milestone inspection hasn't been done. Ask for:
- The 30-day loan contingency back
- The appraisal contingency back
- A contingency for lender approval of the building
- The seller or association to order the questionnaire now

Confirm eligibility with a licensed lender before committing any deposit.

Also worth raising:
- **Price.** $318,500 against PBC condo supply of about 8.5 months and about 86% of PBC condo sales closing under list (Oct 2026, 🟡 docs/01 §1) suggests the buyer has leverage. The WPB condo median of about $210k (Sep 2026, 🟡) is not a like-for-like comparison. Pull building and unit-type comps from BeachesMLS.
- **Concessions.** The seller dropped the buyer's $5,000 request.
- **Board approval timing.**
- **Rental limits**, if the buyer ever plans to rent.

### Questions for the association
1. Has a milestone inspector been hired? What is the Phase 1 date, and has the building department been contacted about the deadline?
2. Does the $2.15M assessment cover the SIRS items or the milestone findings? How would it be paid (installments or a loan)?
3. Under what authority were SIRS reserve contributions paused, and for how long?
4. What is the plan if a storm loss triggers the 5% deductible?
5. Will you complete the lender condo questionnaire, and which lenders have declined the building and why?
6. How many units are delinquent and by how much, and what collection steps are under way?
7. What are the board approval steps and timing for this sale?
8. Please provide 12 to 24 months of minutes, the 2027 proposed budget, and an estoppel certificate.
