## Answer (short version)
- **Month 1 is for setup and building habits, not buying leads.** Spend your 8 hours on your sphere (people you already know), open houses and partner meetings. Hold off on paid ads.
- **Week 1 is setup.** Spend about 3 hours setting up the CRM and loading 150–300 contacts with their consent status recorded. Start sphere outreach that same week.
- **Weeks 2–4 time split:** sphere 3 hrs, one open house 3 hrs, one partner coffee 1 hr, content plus a weekly review 1 hr.
- **Budget split ($300):** open house supplies $80, sphere notes and postage $70, CRM and tools $50 (skip this if your brokerage provides a CRM), unspent reserve $100.
- **Earliest paid test is month 3.** It must be capped at $300 and run at least 60 days. It only starts once your CRM is live, you can reach new leads in under 5 minutes, and a compliance-reviewed lead magnet (a free guide offered in exchange for contact details) is ready.

## Details
**Where the experts differed, and how I settled it**
1. **Sphere volume.** Jordan targets about 50 touches a week. Walter's $70 postage budget covers only about 10 handwritten notes a week (my estimate, unverified). **Settled:** ramp from about 25 touches in week 2 to 50 by week 4. Make up the gap with calls and personal notes rather than paid mail. Texts go only to contacts with recorded consent, and never as mass texts.
2. **Lender seminar share ($40).** Walter budgets a proportional share of a lender seminar. Jordan warns against any payment tied to referrals (RESPA §8). **Settled:** keep the $40 in reserve until your broker or an attorney confirms the arrangement in writing. Month-1 partner meetings are coffee meetings only, with no co-marketing.
3. **When paid ads start.** Jordan says day 31 at the earliest; Walter says month 3. **Settled:** use the stricter date, month 3. Jordan's readiness checks still apply. Because the whole budget is $300, a paid test replaces other spending rather than adding to it.
4. **Time.** Ollie's 3-hour CRM build doesn't fit on top of Jordan's 8-hour plan. **Settled:** in week 1 the CRM build takes the place of the open house and content. From week 2, the 30–45 minute weekly review comes out of the content hour, so content drops to one ZIP or condo explainer every other week.

**CRM basics (Ollie)**
- Pipeline: New → Contacted → Nurture A/B/C → Appointment → Agreement Signed → Closed. Get a signed buyer agreement before any tour.
- Tag contacts by needs only: source, segment, ZIP, price band, property type, timeline.
- Record consent for every contact. Never assume consent for leads from portals.
- Follow-up speed: call new leads within 5 minutes. Call only between 8am and 8pm, and no more than 3 times in 24 hours.

**Scale/stop rule for the later paid test (Jordan)**
- Scale if it produces 2 or more appointments at or below your target cost per appointment. Raise the budget by no more than 25%.
- Stop if it produces no appointments in 60 days, or no signed buyer agreement by day 90.

## Escalations and risks
- **Running out of cash (risk #1).** Example: $18,000 savings ÷ $4,800 a month in total costs ≈ 3.75 months of runway. The first commission check may not come until month 6 or later, which leaves a gap of 2+ months. Aim to have 6–12 months saved.
- **RESPA:** any lender co-marketing or cost-sharing needs written confirmation from your broker or an attorney.
- **Consent:** the open house sign-in sheet's consent language must go through consent-privacy-advisor and compliance-reviewer before you print it. The consent box must start unchecked.
- **Open houses:** get permission from your broker and the listing agent before hosting on brokerage listings.
- **Taxes:** check with a CPA.
- **Conversion figures:** referral conversion of 14–40% is 🔴 unverified, so don't plan around it.
